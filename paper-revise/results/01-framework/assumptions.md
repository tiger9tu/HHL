This ledger defines accounting requirements and records unresolved inputs. It does not validate the manuscript's numerical estimates. `established` labels a mathematical identity or accounting definition, `assumed` a chosen modeling convention, `measured` requires supplied measurements, and `unresolved` denotes an input still needed. ## Error and reliability accounting

There are three distinct uncertainties: approximation error in the answer, probabilistic algorithm/hardware failure, and uncertainty in resource predictions. These must be reported separately. Use a declared output metric and scale. If local errors e_i affect the output with verified Lipschitz/stability factors L_i, then a sufficient budget is `Σ L_i e_i ≤ ε_out`; otherwise retain the nonlinear conversion bound. A relative residual bound does not automatically equal relative solution error, and normalized-state error does not automatically imply accuracy of an unnormalized physical observable. Discretization error belongs in the target budget if the target is a continuum quantity rather than the discrete system's exact output.

The total failure probability may be bounded by the sum of constituent failure bounds without assuming independence. Do not add mutually alternative branch budgets. If B outputs must all be correct, use a batch guarantee (e.g. sum of per-output bounds ≤ δ_batch) or prove a better joint bound. A per-output guarantee alone does not imply that batch guarantee. Expected retries are not deadline guarantees; a cap or tail policy must specify whether incomplete runs count as failures. QEC exposure includes failed attempts and all idle storage during host waits. Heralded rejection and unheralded logical faults are distinct events. A solver with coherent success amplification must declare which success overhead its gate counts already include.

## Cost and energy accounting

Define the workload interval from the agreed initial data state until the requested output is available. Setup includes data generation when required by that initial state, preprocessing, compilation when problem-dependent, factorization/preconditioning, oracle/QRAM build, loading, and hardware initialization attributable to the job. Provide cold-start and warm-start scenarios when either is relevant; do not silently exclude cold-start costs. Do not charge universal software development to just one branch.

For B outputs in G reuse groups, `E_batch = Σ_g E_setup,g + Σ_i E_run,i`. Analogous time addition assumes serial execution; otherwise compute the dependency schedule's makespan. `E_amort = E_batch/B`; never divide peak memory/qubits by B. Classical factorization reuse and quantum input-structure reuse are allowed when technically valid; quantum state preparation generally remains per invocation unless an explicit reusable-state protocol establishes otherwise.

For each mutually exclusive power component j, `E_j = integral P_j(t) dt` in joules. Total operational energy sums those components after allocating shared services. A proposed simple model may use `P = P_fixed + p_qubit Q_reserved + P_dynamic`, but its coefficients must refer to disjoint subsystems; it is not a universal scaling law. Gross energy includes attributed idle power in the window; incremental energy subtracts a specified counterfactual baseline, identically defined for both systems. Report these as different scenarios. Do not multiply measured wall power by PUE again or count cryogenic load and its already-inclusive electrical supply twice. Keep embodied energy outside the operational total. Power x time is valid only for a declared time average or constant power.

## Propagation of model uncertainty

1. Record each input's evidence, unit, supported domain and uncertainty type: rigorous bound, measurement confidence interval, scenario interval, or probability distribution. Scenario intervals have no implied probability. Record correlations, for example between N and κ, distance and physical error rate, memory bandwidth and power, or qubit count and cryostat count.
2. Pass the same uncertain problem instance to both branches. Apply all nonlinear transforms, output-error conversions, repetition rounding, caps, memory limits, and QEC compilation at each admissible joint parameter point. Recheck achieved failure bounds after changing factories, distance or scheduling.
3. Distinguish fixed installed configuration sensitivity from reoptimized configuration sensitivity. The latter re-runs a declared constrained objective and reports selected discrete choices; it is not evidence that an installed machine changes instantly. Preserve infeasible samples as infeasible rather than dropping them.
4. For smooth local behavior only, propagate covariance by `Cov(F) ≈ J Cov(theta) Jᵀ`. Near thresholds, caps, code-distance changes or feasibility boundaries use joint sampling, verified interval bounds or an explicit scenario grid instead. A finite grid is a sensitivity study, not automatically an exhaustive interval enclosure. Sampling requires a justified joint distribution and recorded seed/sample count.
5. Evaluate ratios `S_t = t_c/t_q` and `S_E = E_c/E_q` per joint sample, at the same statistic, workload and energy boundary. Do not take a ratio of means and label it an expected ratio. Ratio bounds `[t_c,min/t_q,max, t_c,max/t_q,min]` for positive rectangular bounds are conservative, not necessarily tight under correlation. Paired joint evaluation is preferable.
6. For a robust advantage over an uncertainty set, the relevant ratio must exceed one throughout the feasible common set; a confidence statement needs the corresponding probabilistic support. Report mixed/infeasible regions and possible multiple crossings rather than forcing one crossover N. A time-energy tradeoff is not simultaneous advantage. No space ratio is defined between bytes and qubits.
