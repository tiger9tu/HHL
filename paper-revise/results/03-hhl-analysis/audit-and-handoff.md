## Experiment protocol and uncertainty

`experiments.py` generates 24 independent matrices per (synthetic family,N,matching-term count), with N∈{8,16,32,64,128} and matching-term count∈{2,4}. `degree` in the CSV is this construction parameter, **not measured row sparsity**; measured `s` and actual `L` are separate columns. The fixed-band family pairs consecutive vertices after a cyclic shift by term index; supports repeat when parity repeats, with independently drawn weights. The random-matching family independently permutes vertices for every term. Edge weights are independent U[−1,1], mirrored to preserve symmetry; every term is one-sparse. Normalize the sum by its exact dense spectral norm. These are synthetic, generally indefinite systems with uncontrolled κ; they are Hamiltonian-simulation diagnostics, not eligible SPD application instances by default.

Five deterministic PDE matrices have m_grid∈{3,5,7,9,11}, N∈{9,25,49,81,121}; their distributions are singletons and have **no sampling confidence interval**. All 485 matrices are evaluated at τ∈{.1,1} and r∈{1,2,4,8}, giving 3880 operator-error measurements. Paired r/time evaluations of the same matrix are correlated. Each matrix's seed is 20260908 plus its zero-based global case index, generated with NumPy RandomState/MT19937; the seed in `samples.csv` suffices to regenerate it with the same configuration and version.

`samples.csv` gives raw errors, commutator-sum bound, bound ratio, N,s,L,κ,normalization, seed, and unitarity residual. `commutators.csv` records all within-matrix pair norms; those pairs are clustered observations, not independent samples. `summary.csv` reports count, mean, median, 5th/95th quantiles, max and a pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap interval for the mean (2000 matrix-level resamples; seed 21260907). Bootstrap intervals with 24 samples are descriptive approximations, not rigorous coverage or a simultaneous extrapolation guarantee. The Figure 9 band shows sample quantiles, not confidence intervals. No fitted asymptotic exponent is reported. Floating-point checks bound neither rounding error rigorously nor unseen matrix behavior; compare their residual scale to the measured errors.

`validation.json` includes commuting exactness, the false B18 tightness example, a cancelling-decomposition stress test, the higher-order coefficient-order regression, and full clock/system HHL validation on an SPD exact-phase-grid instance. Its normalized matrix is .5I+.15Z+.2X, spectrum {.25,.75}, b=(1,1)/√2, m=3, τ_base=2π, reciprocal amplitude min(1,.25/(k/8)) for k>0 and zero for k=0. The ideal successful state exactly equals the dense normalized solve with no clock leakage, probability .2. This is a special exact-grid validation, not a general finite-QPE accuracy theorem.

`pde-validation.csv` verifies the analytic condition number and dense residual for a manufactured smooth solution u=sin(πx)sin(πy)+.1sin(2πx)sin(3πy), f=2π²sin(πx)sin(πy)+1.3π²sin(2πx)sin(3πy), zero Dirichlet boundary. It compares normalized discrete and sampled continuum solutions. It supplies no task-02 scalar-output contract.
