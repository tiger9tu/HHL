# Proposed manuscript addition

We also consider a linear solver using quantum eigenvalue transformation of unitary matrices (QETU), whose input is controlled Hamiltonian evolution. The even-polynomial transformation of Dong, Lin and Tong [1] permits an inverse filter without constructing a SELECT/PREPARE block encoding of the matrix. An affine eigenphase shift and a bounded polynomial approximation of the reciprocal in the transformed spectral variable are necessary; directly applying a reciprocal polynomial to cos(τH/2) would invert the wrong function. Combining bounded reciprocal polynomials [2] with this transformation gives degree d=O(K log(K/ε_x)), where H=A/α_HE has spectrum in [1/K,1] and ε_x is an allocated normalized-state error. K is the inverse gap under the chosen normalization, and need not equal the original condition number.

Our existing application-specific Trotter analysis supplies the microstep error ||H_hat(h)−H||, with P_p(h)=exp(ihH_hat(h)). When every QETU query uses the same P_p(h) and every inverse query uses its exact adjoint, the filter acts on one common effective matrix. Resolvent perturbation and state normalization give a state-error bound 2KΔ/(1−KΔ), where Δ=||H_hat−H|| and KΔ<1. Thus an O(ε_x/K) matrix-error target suffices without multiplying this contribution by d. If the validated application error behaves as a_app h^p, the required number of microsteps per fixed-time query scales as r=O(max{r_branch,τ(a_app K/ε_x)^(1/p)}). Here r_branch enforces a validated local-logarithm range; for the existing first-order bound it is max{1,ceil(2τΣ_a||H_a||)}. A fitted leading BCH coefficient is an empirical estimate unless its remainder and numerical errors are controlled. The alternative per-query telescoping bound remains available when a shared effective matrix cannot be justified.

One inverse-filter attempt uses dr controlled microsteps and O(d) signal rotations. It succeeds with amplitude proportional to ||H⁻¹b_normalized||/K, which may be O(1/K). Repeated postselection therefore costs up to an additional O(K²) factor, while coherent amplitude amplification reduces this to O(K), with corresponding preparation, adjoint and reflection costs. Suppressing logarithmic factors, the amplified evolution contribution is Õ(g_step,p K² max{r_branch,(a_app K/ε_x)^(1/p)}), where g_step,p is the compiled controlled-microstep cost. First-order Trotterization gives Õ(g_step,1 a_app K³/ε_x) when the accuracy-dependent microstep requirement dominates. This direct inverse-filter solver is not an asymptotically optimal QLSA merely because it uses QETU.

The construction reduces dependence on a matrix block-encoding circuit, but does not establish a smaller practical prefactor: term count, decomposition, controlled rotations, arithmetic, synthesis, successful-state preparation and scalar readout must all be costed. Our existing Poisson measurements support control examples, not the variable-conductivity benchmark without a new matched calibration. In that benchmark, conditioning grows proportionally to N at fixed contrast, and the continuum scalar tolerance must first be converted to a phase-aligned state-error budget. Physical resource and crossover claims require a comparison under that same delivered-output contract.

[2] A. Gilyén, Y. Su, G. H. Low and N. Wiebe, *Quantum singular value transformation and beyond: exponential improvements for quantum matrix arithmetics*, STOC 2019, pp. 193–204; [arXiv:1806.01838v1, Section 3.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01838v1).
