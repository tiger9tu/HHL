Comments to the Authors
This manuscript addresses a timely and important question for the quantum 
computing community: how to identify regimes where fault-tolerant quantum 
computing may provide practical advantages over classical computing when 
evaluated not only in terms of runtime, but also in terms of space and energy 
consumption. The authors present a substantial amount of technical work, 
including logical-level resource estimation, fault-tolerant resource estimation using 
surface-code-based methods, and energy modeling for superconducting quantum 
hardware applied to the HHL/QLSA framework. The quantum algorithm analysis 
appears thorough, technically sophisticated, and carefully executed. The 
manuscript also addresses a topic that is becoming increasingly important as the 
community transitions from asymptotic complexity arguments toward practical 
assessments of quantum advantage.
Overall, I believe the paper contains the foundation of a valuable contribution. 
However, I also believe the manuscript currently undersells what may be its most 
signiﬁcant scientiﬁc contribution and would beneﬁt from substantial revision 
before publication.
Major Comment 1: The manuscript's strongest 
contribution is the framework, not the speciﬁc HHL 
case study
The manuscript is presently framed primarily as a resource-analysis study of the 
HHL quantum linear systems algorithm. However, after reading the paper, I found 
the more compelling contribution to be the broader methodology developed by the 
authors.
In particular, the paper combines:
•
algorithmic resource estimation,
•
fault-tolerant compilation and error correction,
•
hardware-level assumptions,

This framework-oriented contribution appears more broadly impactful than the

I am not suggesting that the paper cease to be an HHL study. Rather, I believe the

The HHL analysis can serve naturally as a case study demonstrating the 
methodology.
A revised framing along these lines would also strengthen the paper's broader 
relevance to researchers interested in practical quantum advantage beyond QLSA.
Major Comment 2: The energy model needs greater 
robustness and sensitivity analysis
The energy analysis is one of the most interesting aspects of the paper, but it is also 
the area where I have the greatest concerns.
A signiﬁcant portion of the conclusions rely on assumptions regarding the energy 
cost per physical qubit. While the manuscript appropriately acknowledges that 
these estimates are architecture-dependent and subject to change, many of the 
numerical conclusions are nevertheless presented using speciﬁc ﬁxed values.
My concern is that future architectural improvements could materially alter these 
assumptions. For example, changes in cryogenic design, qubit packaging density, 
control architectures, or cooling-system efﬁciency could signiﬁcantly affect the 
effective energy cost per physical qubit.

Because of this, I strongly encourage the authors to treat energy-per-qubit as a ﬁrst-
class model parameter rather than as a ﬁxed quantity.
More importantly, I believe the paper would beneﬁt substantially from a sensitivity 
analysis that explores the stability of the reported quantum advantage boundaries 
under perturbations of key assumptions.
Examples include:
•
energy cost per physical qubit,
•
physical error rate,
•
logical cycle time,
•
fault-tolerance overhead assumptions,
•
condition number κ,
•
sparsity s,
•
precision ε.
At present, many of the ﬁgures represent point estimates. It is difﬁcult to assess 
how robust the resulting quantum advantage regions are with respect to uncertainty 
in these assumptions.
A systematic sensitivity analysis would signiﬁcantly strengthen the credibility and 
long-term relevance of the conclusions.
Major Comment 3: Practical benchmarking should 
be more closely tied to scientiﬁc computing practice
I do not object to the use of conjugate gradient (CG) as a classical comparison. CG 
is a natural algorithmic counterpart to QLSA and provides a useful theoretical 
baseline.
However, the manuscript frequently discusses practical quantum advantage in the 
context of real-world scientiﬁc computing. In that context, I believe the 
benchmarking discussion should be grounded more carefully in contemporary 
high-performance computing practice.

For example:
•
Which scientiﬁc computing workloads motivate the selected parameter 
regimes?
•
How do representative values of κ, s, and ε compare with those encountered 
in production HPC simulations?
•
How might the conclusions change under realistic preconditioning 
strategies?
•
How do the results compare with state-of-the-art HPC implementations 
rather than desktop-scale assumptions?
The manuscript brieﬂy discusses HPC considerations, but I believe this connection 
should be strengthened.
At minimum, I encourage the authors to justify their selected parameter ranges 
using representative scientiﬁc computing workloads.
More ambitiously, it would be highly valuable if the authors could identify one or 
more canonical application classes (for example, PDE-based simulation, ﬁnite-
element methods, inverse problems, etc.) and evaluate their framework using 
parameter ranges motivated by those workloads.
Doing so would considerably improve the practical relevance of the reported 
quantum advantage boundaries.
Major Comment 4: Clarify audience and 
manuscript positioning
I found the intended audience somewhat unclear.
The introduction begins with a high-level discussion of quantum computing as a 
non-Von-Neumann paradigm beyond Moore's Law, which suggests an audience 
that may not be deeply familiar with quantum computing.

However, the technical portions of the manuscript rapidly transition into detailed 
discussions of HHL, Hamiltonian simulation, fault tolerance, and magic-state 
distillation.
The resulting narrative occasionally feels caught between an introductory article 
and a specialized technical resource-estimation paper.
I encourage the authors to consider more carefully:
•
who the primary audience is,
•
what prior knowledge is assumed,
•
and how the introduction and framing can more naturally connect to the 
technical contributions.
Improving this narrative alignment would make the paper easier to follow and 
increase its impact.
Major Comment 5: Future work and conclusions
The discussion of future work raises many interesting questions. However, I felt 
that the conclusion section could be more focused and more clearly aligned with 
the manuscript's central contribution.
In particular, several future directions appear to follow naturally from the 
framework developed in the paper:
•
application-driven benchmarking,
•
hardware-independent energy modeling,
•
sensitivity and uncertainty quantiﬁcation,
•
extension to additional quantum algorithms,
•
identiﬁcation of quantum advantage regions across diverse application 
domains.
I believe emphasizing these directions would strengthen the conclusion and further 
highlight the broader signiﬁcance of the methodology introduced in the 
manuscript.
